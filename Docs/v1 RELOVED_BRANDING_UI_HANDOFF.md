# 1. Project in one sentence

**reloved is a digital Wall of Kindness that makes giving quality preloved items feel as desirable, modern and frictionless as discovering fashion online — while every item remains completely free.**

The project is charitable/community-led in intent, but it must **never look like a conventional NGO or donation website**.

# 2. Client + project context

**Client / vision owner:** Sheetal Ahuja  
**Technology / design partner:** Totem Interactive  
**Immediate operating support:** Reyansh, Sheetal’s son  
**Launch geography:** Mumbai-led pilot  
**Phase 1 launch milestone:** 20 August 2026  
**Immediate ambition:** Website + Instagram live, initial inventory visible, email/verification setup functional, and the first institutional distribution workflow capable of being facilitated digitally.

The original concept is inspired by the **Wall of Kindness movement in Iran**:

The physical-wall idea is being turned into a digital product.

# 3. What the brand needs to accomplish

The brand has to reconcile several things that can easily conflict:

### It must feel premium
Sheetal repeatedly wants the immediate visual reaction of a high-end fashion platform.

### It must feel generous
This is fundamentally about kindness, giving and passing things forward.

### It must feel youthful and socially shareable
It should work naturally on Instagram and attract people who care about culture, fashion and social impact.

### It must remain dignified
Recipients should never feel like they are browsing a “poor people’s donation site.”

### It must feel local and human
The Wall of Kindness metaphor, neighbourhood activity, donors, institutions and real item stories are core to the concept.

### It must feel operationally credible
Even with expressive art direction, the interface still has to support trust, forms, product details, maps, status, onboarding and institutional coordination.

The identity should therefore feel more like:

**high-fashion editorial × street-art culture × modern social movement**

and less like:

**NGO website × ecommerce template × SaaS dashboard**

# 13. Dynamic graffiti direction

Sheetal explicitly asked for a **dynamic graffiti background**.

Brand work should define the motion language, not only a static wall.

Potential elements:

- Slowly shifting spray forms
- Animated brush stroke reveals
- Paint scribbles that draw on
- Free stamps appearing
- Subtle parallax paper layers
- Small hand-drawn arrows
- Product cut-outs gently moving
- Wall texture responding subtly to scroll
- Logo reveal / paint-on animation

Rules:

- Motion must remain premium
- It must not become a game
- It must not make text hard to read
- It must work on mobile
- It should have a reduced-motion fallback
- The wall must still look intentional when completely static
